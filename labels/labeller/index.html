What each label means
Forest
Natural or degraded forest. Irregular crowns of mixed sizes, uneven texture, no rows or regular clearings. Under the EUDR (FAO) definition, degraded or open forest still counts as forest.
Cocoa
A low, even, fine-textured canopy, often with scattered tall shade trees standing above it. Small clearings, paths and huts nearby. Shaded cocoa can look like thin forest: look for the uniform layer under the big trees.
Other
Oil palm (star-shaped crowns, often in rows), rubber (straight rows, even crowns), food crops, fallow and bush, bare ground, mining pits, buildings, water.
Unsure
You can't tell. Use it freely: a wrong label hurts the model more than a missing one.

Judge the ~30 m box in the centre. If it straddles a boundary, pick the majority and add a note. Check the capture date under the image: if it is years away from 2024–25 and the place looks like it may have changed, say so in a note or use Google Earth's historical imagery.
